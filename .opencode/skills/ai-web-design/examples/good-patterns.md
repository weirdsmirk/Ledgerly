# Good patterns

These examples demonstrate context-led reasoning. The resulting styles can vary widely.

## Earn the pattern

> The inventory screen uses cards only for independently movable kits. Individual parts stay in a compact table because comparison across rows is the main task.

Why it works: containment and layout follow behavior.

## Form a visual premise

> Treat the oral-history archive as an annotated listening desk: transcripts are primary, audio controls remain close to the active passage, and archival metadata sits in a restrained side column.

Why it works: the premise connects atmosphere, hierarchy, and interaction to the content.

## Use familiar composition for a reason

> Center the memorial page's opening statement because it needs a quiet ceremonial pause. Shift the collection below to a left-aligned editorial grid for sustained reading.

Why it works: centering has a job and does not become the only rhythm on the page.

## Use a gradient for meaning

> The weather timeline transitions from the current sky color toward the forecast conditions. Text sits on stable solid surfaces so contrast does not vary with the visualization.

Why it works: the gradient communicates change rather than generic technological energy.

## Make a dashboard product-specific

> Give delayed shipments visual priority, keep route exceptions adjacent to the map, and compress stable summary metrics into the header. Do not allocate equal cards to metrics with unequal operational urgency.

Why it works: hierarchy follows the operator's decisions.

## Preserve a strong existing system

> Reuse the product's square corners, warm gray palette, and condensed labels. Add the new workflow with the same tokens, but relax density in its explanatory first-run state.

Why it works: the change belongs to the product while adapting to a different task.

## Let mobile change the composition

> On narrow screens, place the active recipe step before the ingredient overview, keep the timer sticky, and move secondary notes into a disclosure. The desktop split view remains for cooks who can scan both columns.

Why it works: responsive behavior follows the context of use rather than source order alone.

## Create character without spectacle

> Give the legal research tool an annotated-report feel through narrow measures, precise footnote links, strong evidence labels, and restrained rules. Reserve color for citation status and active filters.

Why it works: distinctiveness comes from content form, type, and detail rather than ornamental effects.

## Review by diagnosis

> The page feels generic because every section repeats a heading, paragraph, and three equal cards. Replace the process section with a continuous annotated sequence, and turn the evidence section into a compact comparison.

Why it works: the revision addresses repeated composition instead of swapping surface styles.
